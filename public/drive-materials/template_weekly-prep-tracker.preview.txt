## Turn the weekly tracker into a feedback loop

Worked example - fictional. Names, organizations, dates, amounts, metrics, and outcomes below are invented to demonstrate the method. Do not copy them as your own experience. Use only facts you can explain and support.

Learning objective: Plan a small amount of deliberate practice, retain evidence, and choose the next exercise from an observed error rather than from the number of resources opened.

## What to record

Choose one primary theme that names a capability, not a resource: for example, explain why marking a node when it enters a BFS queue prevents repeated work. Count an exercise as complete only after an unaided attempt, a test, and a short review. Watching a lesson is preparation; it is not evidence that the capability is reliable.

For each attempt, retain a small artifact: code plus tests, an annotated design, or a two-minute story recording. Record the first decision that went wrong. A useful error is specific enough to change next practice: 'I marked nodes after removal and queued a node twice' is actionable; 'BFS is weak' is not.

## Worked week - fictional

Primary theme: unweighted shortest paths and stable parent links. Planned work: two BFS prompts, one timed re-test, and one spoken explanation. Completed: one relevant lesson, one PDF drill, and three coding attempts. No system-design or behavioral work was planned this week, so those fields are marked 'not planned', not treated as failures.

Evidence: first attempt found the distance but overwrote the parent of an already discovered node. The diamond graph A-B-D and A-C-D exposed duplicate discovery. Correction: mark visited and assign the parent at enqueue time. Re-test: solve an unfamiliar graph with a cycle after 48 hours; return both the shortest distance and one valid path.

Review outcome: the re-test passed correctness and complexity checks, but the explanation did not justify O(V + E). Next practice: give a one-minute edge-counting explanation and test an unreachable destination. Do not repeat the whole lesson just because one explanation needs repair.

## A five-minute weekly review

Ask: What can I now do without notes? Which artifact proves it? Which error appeared twice? Which single exercise will test the correction? Schedule that exercise with a concrete output. Keep volume realistic enough that you can review every attempt.

Use the twelve existing weekly blocks for your own work. Repeated blank blocks are writable tracking space, not twelve lessons. If a metric does not apply, write 'not planned' or 'not applicable'; do not invent a completion count. A missed target should lead to a smaller next plan or a better practice choice, not a fabricated check mark.
