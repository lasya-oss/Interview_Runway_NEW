# Negotiate as an India-Based Remote Hire

_Last reviewed: 2026-07-18_

## Scope and date

This lesson is a dated decision guide for Negotiate as an India-Based Remote Hire, prepared for India-based candidates pursuing remote or US-linked roles. It is educational material, not individualized legal, tax, immigration, employment, or financial advice. Rules, platform availability, fees, and company policies change. Verify the current position on official portals and with a qualified India professional before acting. The useful goal is to know which questions to ask and which records to preserve.

## Start with the engagement model

Separate employee, employer-of-record employee, and independent contractor arrangements. The label in an offer is not enough; actual control, benefits, tax treatment, invoicing, intellectual-property terms, termination rights, and local compliance can differ. Ask which legal entity signs the contract, which country law governs it, who withholds or reports payments, what benefits exist, and whether exclusivity or working-hour requirements apply.

## Map the money path

Write the full payment path from payer to your Indian bank account. Record invoice currency, settlement currency, platform or intermediary, transfer fee, foreign-exchange spread, expected settlement time, refund or chargeback behavior, and the document produced for each payment. Compare total received value and operational reliability, not only the advertised fee. Keep contracts, invoices, bank advice, platform statements, and conversion records together.

## Tax and GST questions

Income classification, deductions, advance-tax obligations, tax return forms, GST registration, export-of-services conditions, place of supply, LUT use, and documentation depend on facts and current law. Do not rely on a social-media checklist as a conclusion. Use the Income Tax Department and GST/CBIC sources as the starting point, then ask a qualified professional to apply them to your engagement. Build calendar reminders from the professional's written advice.

## Contract review

Read compensation, currency, expenses, notice, termination, probation, intellectual property, confidentiality, non-solicit, non-compete, exclusivity, equipment, security, dispute resolution, governing law, and liability together. Highlight clauses that create uncapped personal liability, assign unrelated inventions, allow one-sided changes, delay earned payments, or conflict with another obligation. Ask for revisions in plain language and retain the final signed version.

## Questions to put in writing

Ask the recruiter or contracting contact to confirm the hiring entity, engagement classification, work location, reporting manager, expected overlap hours, on-call duties, pay schedule, invoice process, fee responsibility, expense policy, leave, benefits, notice, termination, and equipment. Ask which version of the policy or contract controls when a sales page, email, and agreement differ. Written answers do not replace professional advice, but they reduce ambiguity and give a reviewer concrete terms to assess.

## Visa and location

A person working physically from India for a foreign organization is not automatically working in the United States. US work authorization questions become relevant when the role requires physical work in the United States or another immigration-triggering activity. The hiring company may still impose location restrictions for payroll, security, export-control, customer, or policy reasons. Ask where you are legally permitted and operationally expected to work; do not infer permission from the word remote.

## Compensation comparison

Normalize offers to annual expected cash in INR after predictable fees, while keeping exchange-rate uncertainty visible. Separate base cash, variable pay, equity, benefits, paid leave, equipment, insurance, tax support, notice protection, and unpaid downtime. Contractor gross pay is not directly comparable to employee cost-to-company. Use multiple market references, but anchor negotiation in role scope, evidence, and alternatives rather than a single online number.

## Records and review cadence

Create one folder per engagement containing the signed agreement, amendments, invoices, payment confirmations, bank records, foreign-exchange documents, expense receipts, tax working papers, professional advice, and key email confirmations. Use consistent filenames with dates and amounts. Reconcile expected and received payments monthly. Review platform terms and official guidance before a material change, and schedule a ninety-day freshness check for tax, GST, payment, immigration, and market claims. Preserve prior versions so a later reviewer can see what information you relied on.

## Operational readiness

Confirm overlap hours, meeting load, on-call expectations, equipment ownership, internet backup, security controls, expense reimbursement, leave approval, performance review, manager location, and communication norms. Test whether the arrangement works during illness, holidays, outages, and urgent incidents. A remote offer is attractive only when the operating model is sustainable and the contract accurately reflects it.

## Decision checklist

Before accepting, obtain the legal entity name, engagement type, total compensation breakdown, payment path, working-location permission, expected hours, leave terms, termination and notice, IP boundaries, equipment/security rules, and dispute process in writing. Have high-risk tax and contract questions reviewed. Keep an offer comparison sheet and a folder for final documents. Recheck official information every ninety days for time-sensitive material. When two sources conflict, prefer the current official authority for the rule and ask the relevant professional to document how it applies to your facts. Record unresolved questions rather than silently choosing the most convenient interpretation.

Use this guide to prepare questions and documents; do not treat it as a personalized conclusion.
