## Write a useful post-interview thank-you

Worked example - fictional. Names, organizations, dates, amounts, metrics, and outcomes below are invented to demonstrate the method. Do not copy them as your own experience. Use only facts you can explain and support.

Learning objective: Reinforce a real point of fit in a brief, accurate message without introducing a second interview answer or pressuring the recipient.

## Choose one detail

Use a detail that actually came from the conversation: a product constraint, an engineering decision, or a team priority. Connect it to one relevant capability. A specific message helps the recipient recognize the discussion; generic praise does not add evidence. Follow the recruiter's communication instructions and do not search for private contact details.

## Worked message - fictional

Subject: Thank you - backend engineer conversation

Hi Arun, thank you for discussing the notification team's reliability work today. Your explanation of separating urgent account alerts from bulk messages helped me understand the role's priorities. My recent work on idempotent delivery and controlled retry queues is relevant to that challenge. I appreciated the discussion and remain interested in the opportunity. Best, Nila.

## Why it works and what to remove

The message references one real discussion point, explains fit in one sentence, and closes without an immediate response request. It does not claim that the interview went well or predict a hiring decision. Remove unsupported enthusiasm, a long project summary, new attachments that were not requested, and repeated questions about feedback.

If you notice a material factual error in your interview answer, correct it clearly and briefly instead of hiding the correction inside praise. Check the recipient's name and role, then send one message through the agreed channel.
