## Ask for a bounded decision extension

Worked example - fictional. Names, organizations, dates, amounts, metrics, and outcomes below are invented to demonstrate the method. Do not copy them as your own experience. Use only facts you can explain and support.

Learning objective: Request enough time for a real unresolved decision while making the date and commitment unambiguous.

## Know what the extra time will resolve

Before asking, identify the remaining diligence: a benefits document, contract review, operating-hours confirmation, or another previously scheduled conversation. Choose the shortest realistic extension. Ask before the deadline and treat a declined request as a real constraint, not as permission to ignore the original date.

## Worked message - fictional

Subject: Request to extend offer decision to 18 September

Hi Riya, thank you again for the offer. I am reviewing the final engagement terms and have a previously scheduled discussion about the team's on-call expectations on 16 September. Would it be possible to extend the decision deadline from 15 September to 18 September? I will provide a firm answer by then and will let you know sooner if I can. I remain enthusiastic about the service-ownership scope. Best, Nila.

## Make the follow-through credible

The reason is truthful, the old and new dates are explicit, and the message does not imply acceptance. In your own request, include the relevant time zone if the cutoff could be ambiguous. Confirm any agreed extension in writing and update your decision calendar.

If the company cannot extend, ask which missing information can be supplied before the original deadline. Then decide using your real constraints. Do not invent another offer, a family emergency, or a professional appointment to create pressure.
